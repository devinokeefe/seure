# BnF ms. NAF 6638: two cipher blocks of February 1559 and their decipherment

## The manuscript

Paris, BnF, Nouvelles acquisitions françaises 6638 is a 19th-century pen copy of a volume now in St Petersburg
(National Library of Russia, Dubrovsky collection, autographs ms. 110): original despatches of the chevalier de
Seure and of Jean Nicot from Portugal, 1559-1561. It is on Gallica: <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525105423>
(page N of the copy is view N+12: p. 29 = view f41).

The copyist reproduced the cipher passages sign by sign. Two pieces contain cipher:

| piece | what | cipher |
|---|---|---|
| "N. 4" | Seure to Henri II, undated and unsigned in the copy [Lisbon, February 1559] | a block of 264 signs on p. 29, at the end of the clear text |
| "N. 5" | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 12 February 1559 (dated and signed after the cipher) | pp. 35-43: 4,284 signs as drawn |

Between them, from the middle of p. 29 to the end of p. 34, the copy has six pages of clear text beginning
"Par ou Vostre majesté peult veoir que l'occasion dont il luy a pleu m'escripre est passée à cause de la venue de la
flotte ...". Margin notes of the copyist ("F. 10a", "F 10b", "F. 11a") show that this text stands on folios 10-11
of the original volume; the cipher of N. 5 is marked as folios 12a to 14a.

## The finding

1. Both blocks are in the cipher of BnF fr. 3151 (the letters of December 1558).
2. The clear text of pp. 29-34 is the decipherment of the two blocks:
   - its first 71 words (324 letters), from "Par ou Vostre majesté" to "laquelle, encores que elle", are the
     content of the N. 4 block;
   - the rest, from "n'apporte riens" to the last words of p. 34, "assez desnuez d'hommes" (1,143 words, 5,266
     letters, including a footnote of the copyist on p. 31), is the content of the N. 5 block.
3. Falgairolle printed this clear text in 1895 as the end of his letter no. 4, after the note "Suivent neuf lignes
   chiffrées", and printed N. 5 with its cipher left undeciphered. He does not say that the text is the content of
   the cipher of either piece, and we take it that he did not see the connection. This rests on a full-text search
   of his edition on Gallica, not on a complete reading of it.

An inference, not checked against the original: since the plaintext of the N. 4 block runs on without a break into
the plaintext of the N. 5 block, "N. 4" and "N. 5" look like two parts of one despatch of 12 February 1559, with a
contemporary decipher sheet (folios 10-11 of the original) bound between them, in the same way as the decipher
sheets of fr. 3151 are bound among the letters they belong to.

## The evidence

The test is a known-plaintext alignment under a **fixed** key from fr. 3151. Nothing is fitted to NAF 6638. The
alignment cost (lower = better fit) of the real text is compared with the cost of the same text with its words in
random order, which keeps the letter frequencies and word lengths. `python code/naf6638_check.py` recomputes
everything in this section ([results/naf6638_check.txt](../results/naf6638_check.txt)).

**N. 4** (264 signs, 70 of them marked uncertain in transcription; key from letter 40 alone, as the test was first
made):

| text aligned with the block | cost |
|---|---|
| the clear text that follows the block | 252.5 (162 of 264 signs on their key value) |
| the same text, words shuffled (20 runs) | 358.6 ± 4.6 (lowest 349.7); z = -23.3 |
| 14 other stretches of the same length from the clear text *before* the block (same letter, same subject) | 369.2 to 383.7 |

**N. 5** (final key from fr. 3151). The three transcription files are joined; 26 signs that the copyist wrote
twice (a dittography on pp. 41-42) are dropped, and three words written in clear among the cipher signs ("pour",
three times) are kept as tokens. That gives 4,261 tokens.

| text aligned with the block | cost |
|---|---|
| the clear text from "n'apporte" to the end of p. 34 | 2856.6 (3,088 of 4,261 tokens on their key value, 72.5%) |
| the same text, words shuffled (20 runs) | 5887.4 ± 10.8 (lowest 5858.8); z = -280.2 |
| the same text without the footnote of p. 31 | 2931.4 (+74.8) |

- The alignment was free to start anywhere in the text; it starts at "n'apporte" by itself.
- Every one of the nine cipher pages, tested alone against the stretch of text that the whole-block alignment
  assigns to it, gives z between -34.4 and -56.9.
- All 5,266 letters are covered. 119 isolated letters have no sign; 142 tokens are aligned with nothing, 88 of
  them signs that are nulls in the key.
- The footnote on p. 31 of the copy belongs to the enciphered text (leaving it out raises the cost by 74.8): the
  copyist moved a passage of the decipherment into a note.
- The last 9 signs of the block have no plaintext.

What the figures do not mean: 72.5% is not a transcription accuracy. The copyist regularised the sign shapes,
several of his shapes stand for more than one sign of the original, and the labels were assigned to his shapes by
convention (see the header of [data/naf6638/cipher/N4_p29.txt](../data/naf6638/cipher/N4_p29.txt)).

## Falgairolle's text against the cipher

Falgairolle's printed text (1895) differs from the copy in a number of places. For each place the alignment was
repeated with his words in place of the copy's. A positive difference means that the cipher fits the copy's reading
better.

| the copy / Falgairolle | difference in cost |
|---|---|
| clause "pour envoyer hors d'Espaigne ... à Barselonne" present / omitted | +41.2 |
| "assez desnuez d'hommes" / "desnuez d'hommes" | +7.0 |
| "mille ou douze cens" / "mil ou onze cens" | +4.4 (douze / onze alone +2.2; mille / mil alone +2.2) |
| "ilz pourroient desmollir" / "ilz porront desmollir" | +2.4 |
| "ne trouveront gueres" / "ne trouverons gueres" | +2.0 |
| "sans soy guieres" / "sans ny gueres" | +1.8 |

The cipher supports the copy in every case tested. Only the first two are beyond doubt: a difference of about 2 is
what one sign more or less on its key value produces, so the last four rows are indications, not proof. The clause
about Barcelona is a passage that Falgairolle's text skips, apparently by a jump of the eye from one "Barselonne"
to the next.

Falgairolle's wording was taken from the full-text search of the Gallica copy of the *Mémoires de l'Académie de
Nîmes* (ark:/12148/bpt6k4863535, views 129-133); the printed page numbers were not checked.

## What it adds to the key

The N. 5 block is 4,261 more signs with known plaintext, half as much again as fr. 3151 offers. Because the copy's
shapes cannot be trusted to match the original classes, it was used only for nine signs whose shape in the copy is
unmistakable. The units aligned with those signs, when they are given no key value of their own, are in
[data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv](../data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv); the conclusions are in
[KEY.md](KEY.md#revised-values-from-naf-6638): `mt` = au, `A` = pour, `P` = que, `V` = h or faict; `t` = m,
`4b` = et and the nulls `14`, `13`, `12` confirmed. The sign-by-sign alignment of the block is in
[results/align_N5.txt](../results/align_N5.txt).

## Limits and open points

- The originals in St Petersburg were not seen. The copy has at least one dittography and does not keep some
  distinctions of the original (for example between the signs labelled `1` and `9`). Whether the original carries
  the decipherment in the same place, and in what hand, is not known to us.
- The transcription of NAF 6638 was made in one pass. A second, independent transcription exists only for
  pp. 35-36 and is not part of this release.
- The values in data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv were read off with a working program that is not part of this
  release (each of the nine signs was left without a key value and took whatever the neighbouring signs left
  over). They are given here as data. The alignment in results/align_N5.txt, made with the fr. 3151 key, allows an
  approximate check.
- Two figures in the working notes are not regenerated by the scripts of this release and are therefore not
  quoted above as results: the cost with a key corrected from the copy itself (2710.0, 74.5% on key value), and the
  cost of keeping the dittography (+43.3).
- Seure's later cipher letters (London, 1560-1561) were not examined.
