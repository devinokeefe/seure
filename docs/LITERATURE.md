# Sources, earlier work, and what was checked

State of knowledge on 2026-10-04. This file says what was consulted, what was not, and what this repository does
and does not add. Anything marked "not consulted" could change the picture.

## Manuscripts

**Paris, BnF, Français 3151** (Gallica <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9059865k>). Letters and papers of
1558. The pieces that concern the cipher, with the folio numbers of the volume:

| no. | fo. | piece | Gallica views |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39 | 71 | Seure to de Fresne, Lisbon, 12 Dec 1558; cipher block on 71r | 72-73 |
| 40 | 73 | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 27 Dec 1558; cipher on 73r-v | 74-75 |
| | 74r | decipherment of the cipher of no. 40, on a separate sheet in a formal hand | 75 |
| 41 | 76-77 | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 12 Dec 1558: a copy of the letter that is no. 44 | 77-78 |
| 43 | 80-82 | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 12 Dec 1558: another copy, almost wholly in cipher | 81-83 |
| 44 | 84, 86 | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 12 Dec 1558; cipher blocks on 84r-v and 86r | 85-87 |
| | 85r-v | decipherment of the cipher of no. 44 (blocks marked A, B, C), bound between the leaves of the letter | 86-87 |

Points on which this description differs from what is found elsewhere:

- The library's online catalogue, as consulted on 2026-10-03, describes no. 41 as a duplicate of no. 40 (and
  no. 43, rightly, as a duplicate of no. 44). No. 41 opens with the same words as no. 44 ("Sire, je vous escripviz par ma derniere lettre du xxviiie d'octobre ...") and is
  dated 12 December in its date line. So the letter of 12 December exists three times (41, 43, 44) and the letter
  of 27 December once (40).
- There are five letters of Seure with cipher, not six: no. 42 (fo. 78) is by another writer.
- The cipher passages were deciphered at the time for nos. 40 and 44, and the decipherments are in the volume.
  Only no. 39 has none.

**Paris, BnF, NAF 6638** (Gallica <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525105423>): 19th-century copy of
St Petersburg, National Library of Russia, Dubrovsky collection, autographs ms. 110. See [NAF6638.md](NAF6638.md).
The St Petersburg original was not seen.

Not examined: BnF Français 15871 (clear letters of Seure from Lisbon, 1559); the archives of Seure's London embassy
of 1560-1561; Simancas; Lisbon.

## Printed and online works

- **Edmond Falgairolle, "Le chevalier de Seure, ambassadeur de France en Portugal au XVIe siècle", *Mémoires de
  l'Académie de Nîmes* XVIII (1895), pp. 49-85** (also as an offprint, Paris, 1896). On Gallica:
  <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k4863535>. A biography and five letters of 1559 from the St Petersburg
  manuscript. Consulted through the full-text search of Gallica, not read through. It contains no letter of
  December 1558 and nothing from fr. 3151. It does not decipher cipher passages (it notes "Suivent neuf lignes
  chiffrées" and the like). It prints, as the end of its letter no. 4, the clear text that
  [NAF6638.md](NAF6638.md) identifies as the content of the cipher.
- **Edmond Falgairolle, *Jean Nicot, ambassadeur de France en Portugal au XVIe siècle: sa correspondance
  diplomatique inédite* (Paris, Challamel, 1897).** The scanned text on the Internet Archive (identifier
  jeannicotambassa00nico) is used here as language-model corpus only.
- **Joaquim Veríssimo Serrão, "Michel de Seure, embaixador francês em Portugal (1557-1559): duas cartas ...",
  *Arquivos do Centro Cultural Português* I (Paris, 1969), pp. 455-458.** **Not consulted**: no copy was accessible online. According to
  Potter (below) it publishes two letters from fr. 3151. Which two, and whether anything is said about the cipher,
  is not known to us. Four pages cannot hold much, and no decipher sheet for letter 39 exists in the volume, so an
  earlier decipherment of letter 39 there is unlikely; **it is not excluded.** The title and page numbers are given
  as cited by Potter and were not verified.
- **David Potter (ed.), *A Knight of Malta at the Court of Elizabeth I: the Correspondence of Michel de Seure, French
  Ambassador, 1560-1561*, Camden Fifth Series 45 (Cambridge, 2014).** Only the introduction and its notes were
  accessible. They cite the letter of fr. 3151, fos 84-87, as "in cipher, with decipher", and quote a sentence of
  the decipherment of fo. 85r. So the existence of the contemporary decipherment of no. 44 is on record in print.
  (The dates given there for this letter, 12 October 1558 and 12 December 1559, appear to be slips for 12 December
  1558.) According to its apparatus, two letters of Seure of March 1560 in that edition survive only as contemporary
  decipherments.
- **Vladimir Chichkine, "Mirage d'une alliance. Les relations franco-portugaises dans les collections manuscrites
  de Saint-Pétersbourg (1557-1572)", *Histoire, économie & société* 2021/1, pp. 38-55.** On the St Petersburg
  collection from which NAF 6638 was copied. The working notes of this project record, from an online check on
  2026-10-04, that the article speaks of the key of Seure's cipher as not found. The article itself could not be
  retrieved again when this file was written; the statement should be verified against the article before it is
  relied on.
- **Luís de Matos, *Les Portugais en France au XVIe siècle* (Coimbra, 1952).** Said by Potter to print Seure's clear
  letters of 1559 from fr. 15871. Not consulted.
- **Daniel Bourdeau, open catalogue of unsolved historical ciphers**
  (<https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/catalogue.html>), checked 2026-10-04. It lists "Seure (Lisbon) to
  de Fresne and Henri II, six letters" as attempted and open, gives about 2,000 signs, and says there is no
  decipherment on the leaves. That entry is what drew attention to the letters. On the three points just named
  the manuscript shows otherwise (five letters; 8,766 signs transcribed here; decipher sheets bound in the volume).
- Satoshi Tomokiyo's *Cryptiana* pages on French ciphers of the period were searched; no mention of Seure's cipher
  was found.

## What is not new here

- That nos. 40 and 44 were deciphered at the time. The sheets have been in the volume since the 16th century, and
  the one for no. 44 is cited by Potter.
- The content of the cipher passages of nos. 40, 41, 43 and 44: anyone can read it from the decipher sheets.
- The text of pp. 29-34 of NAF 6638: Falgairolle printed it in 1895.

## What appears to be new

As far as could be established on 2026-10-04, with the gaps stated above:

- a key for the cipher (which sign stands for what), derived from the decipher sheets;
- a sign-by-sign transcription of the cipher passages;
- a partial reading of the cipher passage of letter 39, for which no decipherment is known;
- the observation that the clear text of NAF 6638 pp. 29-34 is the decipherment of the two cipher blocks of
  February 1559 in that copy, and that those blocks are in the same cipher.

No published key and no published reading of letter 39 were found. That is a statement about a search, not a
proof of absence. In particular Serrão (1969) has not been seen, the St Petersburg originals may carry notes that
the copy omits, and unpublished work would not have been found.
