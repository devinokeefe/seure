# BnF ms. NAF 6638: two cipher blocks and their decipherment

A 19th-century copy of Seure's despatches of 1559 contains two cipher blocks that the 1895 edition left
undeciphered. Their decipherment is in the same copy: the six pages of clear text that stand between the two
blocks.

## The manuscript

Paris, BnF, Nouvelles acquisitions françaises 6638 is a pen copy of a volume now in St Petersburg (National Library
of Russia, Dubrovsky collection, autographs ms. 110): original despatches of the chevalier de Seure and of Jean
Nicot from Portugal, 1559-1561. It is on Gallica: <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b525105423> (page N of
the copy is view N+12, so p. 29 is view 41).

The copyist reproduced the cipher passages sign by sign. Two pieces contain cipher:

| piece | what | cipher |
|---|---|---|
| "N. 4" | Seure to Henri II, undated and unsigned in the copy [Lisbon, February 1559] | a block of 264 signs on p. 29, at the end of the clear text |
| "N. 5" | Seure to Henri II, Lisbon, 12 February 1559 (dated and signed after the cipher) | pp. 35-43: 4,284 signs as drawn |

Between them, from the middle of p. 29 to the end of p. 34, the copy has six pages of clear text beginning
"Par ou Vostre majesté peult veoir que l'occasion dont il luy a pleu m'escripre est passée à cause de la venue de la
flotte ...". The copyist's margin notes ("F. 10a", "F 10b", "F. 11a") show that this text stands on folios 10-11
of the original volume. The cipher of N. 5 is marked as folios 12a to 14a.

## The finding

1. Both blocks are in the cipher of BnF fr. 3151, the letters of December 1558.
2. The clear text of pp. 29-34 is the decipherment of the two blocks.
   - Its first 71 words (324 letters), from "Par ou Vostre majesté" to "laquelle, encores que elle", are the
     content of the N. 4 block.
   - The rest, from "n'apporte riens" to the last words of p. 34, "assez desnuez d'hommes", is the content of the
     N. 5 block: 1,143 words, 5,266 letters, including a footnote of the copyist on p. 31.
3. Falgairolle printed this clear text in 1895 as the end of his letter no. 4, after the note "Suivent neuf lignes
   chiffrées", and printed N. 5 with its cipher left undeciphered. He does not say that the text is the content
   of the cipher of either piece, and we take it that he did not see the connection. This rests on a full-text
   search of his edition on Gallica, not on a complete reading of it.

One inference, not checked against the original: the plaintext of the N. 4 block runs on without a break into the
plaintext of the N. 5 block. So "N. 4" and "N. 5" look like two parts of one despatch of 12 February 1559, with a
contemporary decipher sheet (folios 10-11 of the original) bound between them, just as the decipher sheets of
fr. 3151 are bound among the letters they belong to.

## The evidence

The test is a known-plaintext alignment under a fixed key from fr. 3151. Nothing is fitted to NAF 6638. The
alignment cost (lower is a better fit) of the real text is compared with the cost of the same text with its words
in random order, which keeps the letter frequencies and word lengths. `python code/naf6638_check.py` recomputes
everything in this section ([results/naf6638_check.txt](../results/naf6638_check.txt)).

N. 4 (264 signs, 70 of them marked uncertain in transcription; key from letter 40 alone, as the test was first
made):

| text aligned with the block | cost |
|---|---|
| the clear text that follows the block | 252.5 (162 of 264 signs on their key value) |
| the same text, words shuffled (20 runs) | 358.6 ± 4.6 (lowest 349.7); z = -23.3 |
| 14 other stretches of the same length from the clear text *before* the block (same letter, same subject) | 369.2 to 383.7 |

N. 5 (final key from fr. 3151). The three transcription files are joined. 26 signs that the copyist wrote twice (a
dittography on pp. 41-42) are dropped, and three words written in clear among the cipher signs ("pour", three
times) are kept as tokens. That gives 4,261 tokens.

| text aligned with the block | cost |
|---|---|
| the clear text from "n'apporte" to the end of p. 34 | 2856.6 (3,088 of 4,261 tokens on their key value, 72.5%) |
| the same text, words shuffled (20 runs) | 5887.4 ± 10.8 (lowest 5858.8); z = -280.2 |
| the same text without the footnote of p. 31 | 2931.4 (+74.8) |

- The alignment was free to start anywhere in the text. It starts at "n'apporte" by itself.
- Each of the nine cipher pages, tested alone against the stretch of text that the whole-block alignment assigns
  to it, gives z between -34.4 and -56.9.
- All 5,266 letters are covered. 119 isolated letters have no sign. 142 tokens are aligned with nothing, 88 of
  them signs that are nulls in the key.
- The footnote on p. 31 of the copy belongs to the enciphered text: leaving it out raises the cost by 74.8. The
  copyist moved a passage of the decipherment into a note.
- The last 9 signs of the block have no plaintext.

The 72.5% is not a transcription accuracy. The copyist regularised the sign shapes, several of his shapes stand for
more than one sign of the original, and the labels were assigned to his shapes by convention (see the header of
[data/naf6638/cipher/N4_p29.txt](../data/naf6638/cipher/N4_p29.txt)).

## Falgairolle's text against the cipher

Falgairolle's printed text differs from the copy in a number of places. For each one the alignment was repeated
with his words in place of the copy's. A positive difference means that the cipher fits the copy's reading better.

| the copy / Falgairolle | difference in cost |
|---|---|
| clause "pour envoyer hors d'Espaigne ... à Barselonne" present / omitted | +41.2 |
| "assez desnuez d'hommes" / "desnuez d'hommes" | +7.0 |
| "mille ou douze cens" / "mil ou onze cens" | +4.4 (douze / onze alone +2.2; mille / mil alone +2.2) |
| "ilz pourroient desmollir" / "ilz porront desmollir" | +2.4 |
| "ne trouveront gueres" / "ne trouverons gueres" | +2.0 |
| "sans soy guieres" / "sans ny gueres" | +1.8 |

The cipher supports the copy in every case tested. Only the first two are beyond doubt: a difference of about 2 is
what one sign more or less on its key value produces, so the last four rows are indications. The clause about
Barcelona is a passage that Falgairolle's text skips, apparently by a jump of the eye from one "Barselonne" to the
next.

Falgairolle's wording was taken from the full-text search of the Gallica copy of the *Mémoires de l'Académie de
Nîmes* (ark:/12148/bpt6k4863535, views 129-133). The printed page numbers were not checked.

## What it adds to the key

The N. 5 block is 4,261 more signs with known plaintext, half as much again as fr. 3151 offers. Because the copy's
shapes cannot be trusted to match the original classes, it was used only for nine signs whose shape in the copy is
unmistakable. The conclusions are in [KEY.md](KEY.md#revised-values-from-naf-6638): `mt` = au, `A` = pour,
`P` = que, `V` = h or faict; `t` = m, `4b` = et and the nulls `14`, `13`, `12` confirmed. The units aligned with
those nine signs are in [data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv](../data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv), and the sign-by-sign
alignment of the block is in [results/align_N5.txt](../results/align_N5.txt).

## Limits and open points

- The originals in St Petersburg were not seen. The copy has at least one dittography and does not keep some
  distinctions of the original (for example between the signs labelled `1` and `9`). Whether the original carries
  the decipherment in the same place, and in what hand, is not known to us.
- NAF 6638 was transcribed in one pass. A second transcription exists only for pp. 35-36 and is not included.
- The program that produced data/key/naf6638_n5_values.tsv is not included; the values are given as data. The
  alignment in results/align_N5.txt, made with the fr. 3151 key, allows an approximate check.
- Seure's later cipher letters (London, 1560-1561) were not examined.
